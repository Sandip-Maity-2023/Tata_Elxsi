### **1. Document Overview & Abstract**

* **Title:** Damage Propagation Modeling for Aircraft Engine Run-to-Failure Simulation


* **Authors:** Abhinav Saxena (NASA Ames / RIACS), Kai Goebel (NASA Ames), Don Simon (NASA Glenn), and Neil Eklund (GE Global Research).


* **Core Objective:** Describes how damage propagation can be modeled within aircraft gas turbine engine modules using the Commercial Modular Aero-Propulsion System Simulation (C-MAPSS) tool. It outlines the generation of run-to-failure challenge data for the Prognostics and Health Management (PHM'08) data competition.

### **2. Key Sections & Technical Details**

#### **I. Introduction**

* **The Problem:** Data-driven prognostics suffers from a lack of public run-to-failure datasets because real-world fault progression data is time-consuming, expensive, and often withheld for proprietary reasons.


* **Solution:** Develop a simulation-based approach to generate realistic damage propagation data for algorithmic training and testing.



#### **II. Prognostics Definition**

* **Remaining Useful Life (RUL):** Defined exclusively as the estimation of remaining useful component life in units of time (hours or cycles).


* **End-of-Life (EOL):** Determined by user-specified, measurable operational thresholds.



#### **III. System Model (C-MAPSS)**

* **Simulation Environment:** Uses **C-MAPSS** (Commercial Modular Aero-Propulsion System Simulation), coded in MATLAB/Simulink, simulating a 90,000 lb thrust class large commercial turbofan engine.


* **Capabilities:** Atmospheric model (altitudes up to 40,000 ft, Mach numbers 0–0.90, temperatures $-60^{\circ}\text{F}$ to $103^{\circ}\text{F}$), power-management systems, and a built-in control system with fan-speed controllers and limit regulators.


* **Inputs (14 total):** Includes fuel flow ($W_f$) and 13 health-parameter modifiers across 5 rotating components (Fan, LPC, HPC, HPT, LPT).


* **Outputs (21 variables selected out of 58):** Includes sensor response surfaces and operability margins (e.g., $T_2, T_{24}, T_{30}, T_{50}, P_2, P_{15}, P_{30}, N_f, N_c, \text{epr}, \text{Ps30}, \text{phi}, \text{NRc}, \text{BPR}, \text{farB}, \text{htBleed}$).



#### **IV. Damage Propagation Modeling**

* **Existing Reliability Models Reviewed:**
* *Arrhenius Model* ($t_f = A e^{\frac{\Delta H}{kT}}$): For chemical, diffusion, or thermal migration failure modes.


* *Coffin-Manson Mechanical Crack Growth Model* ($N_f = A f^{-\alpha} \Delta T^{-\beta} G(T_{max})$): For mechanical fatigue and solder/metal deformation under thermal cycling.


* *Eyring Model* ($t_f = \frac{A}{T} e^{(\Phi + \frac{\Delta H}{kT})} [ \sinh(\frac{B+C/T}{2} S_1) ]^{-1} \dots$): For stress and temperature interactions.




* **Challenge Damage Model Formulation:** Assumes an exponential wear rate $w = Ae^{B(t)}$ to capture macro-level degradation.


* **Health Index ($h(t)$):** Defined as $h(t) = 1 - d - \exp\{a t^b\}$.


* **Module-Specific Indices:** Flow ($f(t)$) and efficiency ($e(t)$) are modeled separately and aggregated into an overall health index $H(t) = g(e(t), f(t))$, where $g$ represents the minimum of all operative margins (Fan, HPC, LPT, and EGT margins). Failure occurs when $H(t) = 0$.





#### **V. Application Scenario & Data Generation**

* **Operational Variability:** Tracks engines across diverse flight conditions (Altitude: 0–42K ft, Mach: 0–0.84, Throttle Resolver Angle (TRA): 20–100).


* **Noise Contamination (Multi-stage):**
* *Initial Wear:* Modeled via initial $e_0, f_0$ parameters bound within 1% degradation ($[0.99, 1]$).


* *Process Noise:* Trajectory parameters ($a_k, b_k$) combined with a mixture of random distributions to account for manufacturing, assembly, and between-flight maintenance (making the degradation non-monotonic).


* *Measurement Noise:* Random noise added to all output sensor channels.




* **Data Generation Steps:**
1. Choose initial deterioration.


2. Impose exponential flow/efficiency loss rates constrained by bounds ($a_k \in [0.001, 0.003]$, $b_k \in [1.4, 1.6]$).


3. Stop simulation when health $H = 0$.


4. Superimpose sensor measurement noise.





#### **VI. Competition Data Structure**

* **Datasets Created:** Divided into Training, Test, and Validation sets.


* *Training Set:* Trajectories run until the failure threshold ($H = 0$).


* *Test Set:* Trajectories pruned prior to failure (RUL ranging from 10 to 150 cycles).


* *Validation Set:* Pruned with a wider RUL range (6 to 190 cycles) to test algorithm robustness.





#### **VII. Performance Evaluation & Metrics**

* **Asymmetric Scoring Function:** Because early predictions are preferred over late predictions in aircraft engine maintenance, the scoring metric penalizes late predictions exponentially heavier than early predictions:


* $S = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (e^{-\frac{d}{a_1}} - 1)$ for $d < 0$ (Early prediction)


* $S = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (e^{\frac{d}{a_2}} - 1)$ for $d \ge 0$ (Late prediction)


* Where $d = \hat{t}_{\text{RUL}} - t_{\text{RUL}}$, $a_1 = 10$, and $a_2 = 13$.




* **Correlation Metric:** An auxiliary correlation metric is recommended alongside the aggregated error score to verify that an algorithm performs consistently across all Units Under Test (UUTs) rather than excelling on some while failing on others.



#### **VIII. Conclusions**

* C-MAPSS successfully provides a reliable, physics-inspired platform for simulating gas turbine degradation and generating robust benchmark run-to-failure datasets for PHM research communities.
